Pipe: Parent and child can send bytes through the kernel.

Shared Memory: Both can write/read to the same memory region.

Message Queue: They can send structured messages safely.

Socket: They can even communicate across systems.


Think in Real-World Terms

	When you practice, keep asking:

	“Is this one-way or two-way communication?”

	“Does data persist after process exits?”

	“How does the kernel transfer data — copy or reference?”

	“Which IPC fits best if processes are on different machines?”

	That kind of curiosity turns practice into mastery.

Pro Tips

	Always code small, compile with -Wall -Wextra.

	Use separate terminals for sender and receiver.

	Observe kernel resources (ipcs, /proc, netstat) after running.

	After you finish one IPC type, summarize in a note:

	“How data moves, what syscalls used, pros/cons.”
